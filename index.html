Three goals, one household problem

The UN Sustainable Development Goals Report 2026 shows that water stress is not improving, clean energy is growing too slowly, and emissions are still rising. Households in water-scarce countries like Egypt sit where all three meet.

Clean water and sanitation

  • 2.1 billion people lack safely managed drinking water.
  • Global water stress is stuck at 18%, nearly the same as 2015.
  • In Northern Africa and Western Asia it is above 75%.

Affordable and clean energy

  • Renewables supply only 18% of final energy use.
  • Electricity is the leading sector, at just over 30% renewable.
  • Energy-intensity gains slowed to 1.5% in 2023, below target.

Climate action

  • 2024 reached 1.55 °C above pre-industrial levels.
  • Current pledges still lead to 2.3–2.5 °C of warming.
  • A hotter climate means more droughts and higher water demand.

Feedback changes behaviour, if it is specific

We reviewed peer-reviewed studies on household water and energy use, feedback tools and Egypt’s water situation. These are the findings that shaped our design.

8%

Lasting water savings from smart-meter feedback. About 47% of 334 households in Valencia kept using 8% less water more than two years later.

Cominola et al., 2021
~10%

Lower carbon footprint after using a calculator. Users cut emissions about 10% in the first weeks, then the effect faded without follow-up.

Enlund et al., 2023
42

Studies in a feedback meta-analysis. Feedback reduces energy use overall. Frequent feedback combined with goals works best.

Karlin et al., 2015
6.3%

Savings for the highest users when they were compared with their neighbours, versus 2.0% on average.

Allcott, 2011
23%

Of footprint-calculator users felt they knew how to act. 91% learned something and 78% felt motivated, but most lacked concrete steps.

Collins et al., 2020
10%

Possible drop in Nile inflow to Lake Nasser by 2095 in one projection, though models disagree. Upper Egypt is already water-scarce.

Ashour et al., 2024

The gap we target: most calculators tell people their footprint but not what to do next. AquaWatt turns your numbers into a ranked list of actions, each with its own water, energy, CO₂ and money savings, and lets you build a personal plan.

How we calculate (assumptions)
  • Standard shower head 9 L/min, low-flow 6 L/min; 60% of shower water is heated by 25 °C.
  • Electric heater: 0.032 kWh per heated litre (90% efficient). Gas heater: 0.0073 kg CO₂ per heated litre. Solar: about zero.
  • Brushing with the tap running: 24 L per person per day; with the tap off: 2 L. Toilets 36 L and kitchen/drinking 25 L per person per day.
  • Laundry: 60 L and 0.9 kWh per warm load; 0.4 kWh for a cold full load, with about 20% fewer loads.
  • Air conditioner: 1.1 kW average draw; about 4% less energy for each °C higher, up to 25 °C.
  • Bulbs on 5 h/day; LED 9 W, older bulbs 25 W on average. Other appliances (fridge, TV, router): 120 kWh/month.
  • Water supply and treatment: 0.6 kWh per m³. Grid emission factor about 0.47 kg CO₂/kWh (approximate Egypt average). 30-day month.
  • These are estimates for awareness, not a bill. Your real numbers depend on your appliances and habits.

        How we built it

        Simple, private, works on any phone

        1. Research

          We chose SDG 6, 7 and 13 from the UN SDG Report 2026 and reviewed peer-reviewed papers from Google Scholar and ScienceDirect.

        2. Model

          We turned the research into a simple model of household water, electricity and CO₂ use, with every assumption listed on the page.

        3. Build

          One web page in HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No sign-up, no data collection, and it adapts to phone screens and dark mode.

        4. Verify

          We used AI tools to help summarise papers and check code, then checked every number against the original sources.
